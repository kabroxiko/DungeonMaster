THE TABLE'S MAP — this character lives on it

The world this table plays on has already been generated by the map service, once, and it never changes. Every
character at the table is written onto it. The regions and named places listed below are that world: they are the only
geography this character sheet may use.

- **Homeland.** Growing up somewhere is part of a life story, so `brief_backstory` must place the character's origin on
  this map: in one of the **regions** below (write "born in the region of X", "raised on the borders of X") or in one of
  the named **settlements**.
- **Every place you name is one of the named places below**, spelled exactly as written. Never invent a settlement,
  town, village, stronghold, temple or market, and never rename, translate, respell or "correct" a name from this list:
  the players can read these names on their own map, and a different one contradicts it.
- **Use each name as the kind it is.** A region is a territory — say "the region of X", never "the city of X" or "the
  market of X". Only settlements have streets, gates, docks and markets. Lakes, rivers and mountains have no buildings.
- **A home is in one region.** Each named place states the region it belongs to (“— in X”). A character raised in that
  place is from that region, and the sheet must not move them to another one.
- **Surroundings are part of where the character grew up.** Each named place reports the ground around it: its terrain,
  the terrain bordering it, and whether the coast, a river reaching it or only a river near it, a lake, mountains or
  an island are at hand. A character raised on the coast knows boats and tides; one from a river town knows floods and
  ferries; one raised within a walk of a river it does not touch knows that river only as a landmark, not as a living;
  one from the mountains knows snow and thin air; one from an island that reports the coast uses boats to leave it,
  while one raised inland on an island rode to its shore. Use what the place reports and never
  contradict it: a desert homeland has no river, woods or harbour, so give it wells, cisterns or caravan roads instead.
- **If the story needs somewhere unnamed**, describe it relative to a named place — "a farmstead an hour outside
  <place>", "a shrine up the valley from <place>" — instead of inventing a proper name.
- **Do not add geography.** No countries, coastlines, islands, roads or mountain ranges beyond the ones listed, and no
  second continent: the character comes from somewhere on this map or from nowhere.
- **This character is unaware of the campaign.** Naming a home region or town is required; describing the campaign's
  plot, factions or hidden objective is not.
- **Names keep their spelling.** These place names are generated in the table's language. When the sheet is written in
  another language, the prose is translated but the map's names are copied verbatim — this overrides the general
  instruction that every string on the sheet is in one language.
